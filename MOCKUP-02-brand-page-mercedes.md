## About Mercedes-Benz Engines

Mercedes-Benz organises its engines into two code families: OM for diesels and M for petrols. Since 2000, the range has included the OM646 and OM651 four-cylinder diesels fitted across C-Class, E-Class and van models, the OM642 V6 diesel used from the E-Class through to the Sprinter, and petrol families including the M271 four-cylinder and M272 V6. From 2016 the OM654 and OM656 introduced the current diesel generation, while AMG models run their own line from the M156 6.2 V8 to today's M177 4.0 biturbo.

Two things matter when buying a replacement. First, Mercedes engines are application-specific: the same family can differ in sump, turbo arrangement and ancillaries between a saloon, an SUV and a van, so we sell configured units, not generic engines. Second, several modern Mercedes engines are Renault-Nissan sourced — the 1.5 diesel in the A-Class is the Renault K9K and the 1.6 diesel in the C-Class and Vito is the Renault R9M — which we list under their most widely traded names with every Mercedes code cross-referenced, so the engine you find is always the engine that fits.
